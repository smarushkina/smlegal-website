Company Formation in Bulgaria

Setting up a Bulgarian limited liability company — quickly, with precise documents and no surprises. We also assist foreign founders, including remotely.

The limited liability company — the OOD, or the single-member EOOD — is the most common form of doing business in Bulgaria. Shareholders are not personally liable for the company's debts, and since the introduction of the euro the minimum share capital is a symbolic EUR 1. An EOOD has a single shareholder, which may be an individual or a company; an OOD has two or more.

Behind a seemingly simple registration lie decisions that shape how the company will operate for years: who manages it and how, how profits are distributed, and what happens when a shareholder leaves or shareholders disagree. SM Legal prepares documents that meet the Commercial Register's requirements while protecting your interests.

What to decide before incorporation

  • Legal form — EOOD, OOD or another form, such as a variable capital company for start-ups with investors;
  • Company name — we check availability and can reserve it in the Commercial Register;
  • Registered office — an address in Bulgaria for official correspondence;
  • Scope of business — including whether any activity requires a licence or registration;
  • Capital and shares — the amount of capital and how it is divided;
  • Management — one or more managers and how the company is represented.

The steps

1. Consultation and structure

We discuss your business goals, the shareholders and growth plans, and recommend the right form and structure.

2. Constitutional documents

We draft the memorandum of association (for an EOOD) or the articles of association and the minutes of the founding meeting (for an OOD), together with the manager's declarations and the beneficial ownership details required under anti-money-laundering legislation.

3. Notarisation and capital

The manager's consent and specimen signature are notarised. The capital is paid into a bank escrow account, and the bank issues a certificate of payment.

4. Filing with the Commercial Register

We file the application electronically with the Commercial Register at the Registry Agency, which reduces the state fee and speeds up the process.

5. Registration and next steps

Once registered, the company receives its UIC (EIK) and can start trading. We assist with the next steps — a current bank account, VAT registration where required, registration of premises and the first employment contracts.

Timing and costs

The Registry Agency decides on the application within a few working days. With documents ready and capital paid, the whole process — from the first consultation to registration — is usually completed within one to two weeks. Costs comprise the state registration fee, the notary fee and our fee; we provide an exact estimate in advance.

Foreign founders

Foreign individuals and companies may form and own companies in Bulgaria, and foreign nationals may act as managers. Documents issued abroad must be legalised or apostilled and translated into Bulgarian. Under a power of attorney, we can carry out much of the procedure without the founder having to travel to Bulgaria.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum share capital of an EOOD or OOD?

Since the introduction of the euro on 1 January 2026, the minimum share capital of an EOOD or OOD is EUR 1. A higher amount may be appropriate depending on the business and the expectations of partners.

How long does it take to register a company in Bulgaria?

The Registry Agency decides within a few working days. With documents ready and capital paid, the whole process usually takes one to two weeks.

What is the difference between an EOOD and an OOD?

An EOOD is formed and owned by a single shareholder, while an OOD has two or more. In an OOD, the articles of association also govern the relationship between shareholders — profit distribution, share transfers and decision-making.

Can a foreigner register a company in Bulgaria?

Yes. Foreign individuals and companies can form companies in Bulgaria and act as managers. Foreign documents must be legalised or apostilled and translated into Bulgarian.

Does the company have to register for VAT?

VAT registration is mandatory once turnover reaches the statutory threshold and, in certain cases, regardless of turnover — for example for some supplies involving other EU Member States. We advise you on when and how to register.
