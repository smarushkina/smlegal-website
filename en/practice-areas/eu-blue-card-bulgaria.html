EU Blue Card in Bulgaria

End-to-end legal support for employers and highly qualified non-EU professionals — from the eligibility assessment to obtaining and renewing the card.

The EU Blue Card is a combined residence and work permit for highly qualified professionals who are not EU nationals. In Bulgaria it is governed by the Labour Migration and Labour Mobility Act and the Foreigners in the Republic of Bulgaria Act, which transpose Directive (EU) 2021/1883. For employers, the Blue Card is usually the fastest and most predictable way to hire a specialist from a third country.

SM Legal combines immigration and employment law expertise and manages the whole process: eligibility assessment, employment contract and job description, documents, filing, communication with the authorities and subsequent renewal.

Who is eligible

  • Qualification — a completed higher education degree or, for certain information and communication technology occupations, proven professional experience relevant to the position;
  • Employment contract — for highly qualified employment in Bulgaria, for at least the minimum duration required by law;
  • Salary — not lower than the statutory threshold, which is linked to the national average gross salary and updated periodically;
  • Documents — a valid passport, diplomas (recognised or accompanied by a certificate where required), health insurance and accommodation.

Because the salary threshold changes periodically, we check the current amount and the proposed salary before every application.

The steps

1. Eligibility assessment

We review the candidate's education and experience, the position and the salary, and draw up a timetable for every step. At this stage we also tell you whether the Blue Card is the right route or whether another permit, such as the single permit, is more suitable.

2. Employment contract and documents

We draft the employment contract and job description to meet the requirements for highly qualified employment and prepare a checklist of documents for the employer and the employee — including legalisation or apostille and translation of foreign documents.

3. Filing the application

We file the application with the Migration Directorate of the Ministry of Interior. The Employment Agency gives an opinion on the employer and the terms of employment.

4. Decision and type D visa

Following a positive decision, an employee who is outside Bulgaria obtains a long-stay (type D) visa from the Bulgarian embassy or consulate.

5. Issuing the card and starting work

After arriving, the employee applies for the card itself. Work may begin only once the permit has been obtained — starting earlier is one of the most common and costly mistakes.

How long it takes

Realistically, the whole procedure takes several months, depending on document preparation, the authorities' workload and visa processing times. We therefore recommend starting as soon as the candidate has been selected.

Advantages over the single permit

A labour market test is generally not required for the Blue Card. Holders benefit from more favourable conditions when changing employer after an initial period, for family reunification and for mobility within the EU, as well as a faster route to long-term residence.

Frequently asked questions

What salary is required for an EU Blue Card in Bulgaria?

The gross salary must be at least the statutory threshold, which is linked to the national average gross salary and updated periodically. We check the current threshold before every application.

How long does it take to obtain a Blue Card?

Realistically, several months for the whole procedure, depending on document preparation, the authorities’ workload and the issuing of a type D visa.

Is a labour market test required?

A labour market test is generally not required for the EU Blue Card, unlike the general single permit procedure.

Can the employee’s family move to Bulgaria?

Yes. Blue Card holders benefit from facilitated family reunification. We also assist with permits for family members.

Can the employee start work before the card is issued?

No. Work may begin only after the permit has been obtained. Starting earlier exposes both the employer and the employee to risk.
