Transferring Shares in a Bulgarian EOOD or OOD: Documents, Steps and Costs

A practical guide to selling, gifting or transferring shares between shareholders — the documents required, how the procedure works and what to check in advance.

Share transfers are among the most common corporate changes in Bulgaria. They arise when a business is sold, an investor comes in, a shareholder exits or a family restructures its holdings. The transaction looks simple, but the law prescribes a strict form. Failing to observe it makes the transfer void, and gaps in the paperwork lead to a refusal of registration.

This article sets out the steps, the documents and the main risks when transferring shares in a limited liability company (OOD) and in a single-member limited liability company (EOOD).

1. Legal framework

Share transfers are governed by Article 129 of the Bulgarian Commerce Act. A share may be transferred and inherited. The transfer agreement must be in writing, with the signatures and the content notarised simultaneously. This form is a condition of validity: an agreement without it is void.

The same form applies to a power of attorney if a party is represented. This matters especially for foreign parties: a power of attorney notarised abroad must meet the requirements of Bulgarian law to be accepted by the Bulgarian notary.

2. Transfers between shareholders and to third parties

Between shareholders of an OOD

When a share is transferred to an existing shareholder, no admission of a new member is needed. The articles of association may, however, impose additional conditions such as prior consent or a right of first refusal, so we always review the current articles before a transaction.

To a third party in an OOD

A share may be transferred to an outsider only if the general meeting admits that person as a shareholder. The resolution requires a majority of more than three quarters of the capital, unless the articles require a larger majority. Without it, the transfer will not be registered.

In an EOOD

In an EOOD, decisions are taken by the sole owner of the capital. When the entire capital is transferred, the company remains an EOOD with a new memorandum of association. When only part of the capital is transferred, the EOOD becomes an OOD and articles of association are adopted.

3. The steps

Step 1: Checking the company

Before the transaction, the buyer should check the company — registered details, financial position, tax and social security liabilities, key contracts and any litigation. For larger deals we recommend a legal due diligence review.

Step 2: Shareholder resolution

For a transfer to a third party, the general meeting admits the new shareholder and, where needed, amends the articles. If the manager changes, the resolution can cover that too.

Step 3: Agreement and notarisation

The agreement sets out the parties, the share, the price and payment terms, as well as liability, warranties and the handover of records. The notary certifies the signatures and content at the same time. The law also requires declarations, including on salaries and social security contributions paid to the company's employees.

Step 4: Commercial Register filing

The change is filed with the Commercial Register at the Registry Agency, together with the resolution, the agreement, the updated articles or memorandum and the other required documents. Filing electronically reduces the state fee.

Step 5: Beneficial owners

If the transfer changes the individuals who control the company, the new beneficial ownership details must be declared. Missing this is one of the most common grounds for penalties under anti-money-laundering legislation.

4. Documents required

  • share transfer agreement with signatures and content notarised simultaneously;
  • general meeting resolution (OOD) or sole owner's decision (EOOD);
  • updated articles or memorandum of association;
  • declarations required by the Commerce Act and the Commercial Register Act;
  • application for registration and beneficial ownership details;
  • powers of attorney in the required form, where parties are represented;
  • legalised or apostilled and translated documents for foreign parties.

5. Costs and tax

Costs comprise the notary fee, which depends on the value of the transaction, the state registration fee and our legal fee. We can give an exact estimate once the price and structure of the deal are known.

When an individual sells shares, the gain between the sale price and the acquisition cost is taxable income under the Personal Income Tax Act and is declared in the annual tax return. When the seller is a company, the result is reflected in its corporate taxation. Gifts and transactions between related parties may have further tax consequences, so the structure should be discussed in advance.

6. Common mistakes

  • An agreement without simultaneous notarisation of signatures and content — the transfer is void;
  • A transfer to a third party without a general meeting resolution admitting them;
  • A power of attorney in the wrong form, especially when notarised abroad;
  • Ignoring conditions in the articles, such as a right of first refusal;
  • Unchecked company liabilities that the buyer discovers after closing;
  • Failing to declare the new beneficial owners.

Frequently asked questions

What form must a share transfer agreement take in Bulgaria?

It must be in writing, with the signatures and the content notarised simultaneously. This form is a condition of validity — an agreement without it is void.

Is a shareholder resolution required?

Yes, when the share is transferred to someone who is not a shareholder. The general meeting must admit them by a majority of more than three quarters of the capital, unless the articles require a larger majority.

How long does a share transfer take?

With documents ready, notarisation takes one day and the Registry Agency decides on the application within a few working days.

Is tax payable on the sale of shares?

When the seller is an individual, the gain between the sale price and the acquisition cost is taxable income declared in the annual tax return. When the seller is a company, the result is reflected in its corporate taxation.

Can shares be transferred through an attorney-in-fact?

Yes, but the power of attorney must have the same form as the agreement — with signature and content notarised. A power of attorney notarised abroad should be checked in advance.
