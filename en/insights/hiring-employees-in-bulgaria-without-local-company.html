Hiring Employees in Bulgaria without a Local Company: EOR, Temporary Agency Work or Your Own Company?

Three models for a foreign company hiring employees in Bulgaria — Employer of Record (EOR), a temporary work agency and its own company — and how to choose between them.

Foreign companies wishing to do business in Bulgaria can choose between different models for hiring and managing local employees. In some cases a company can start working with a team in Bulgaria without setting up its own company. In others, establishing a local legal entity may provide a more suitable and sustainable structure.

The possible solutions include using an Employer of Record (EOR), hiring employees through a temporary work agency, or setting up the company's own Bulgarian entity.

Each of these options has a different legal framework, allocation of responsibilities and practical consequences. The choice should therefore reflect the nature of the activity, the number of employees and the company's plans for its presence on the Bulgarian market.

1. Hiring through an Employer of Record (EOR)

Employer of Record is a model in which a local provider concludes employment contracts with employees in Bulgaria and takes on the formal obligations of an employer.

The provider is usually responsible for the employment documentation, payroll, taxes and social security contributions, as well as for administering leave and other employment rights.

The foreign company usually sets the employees' business tasks and operational priorities without itself being a party to their employment contracts.

Advantages

EOR can allow a company to start working with local employees more quickly, without immediately setting up a Bulgarian company. The model can reduce the administrative burden and provide access to local expertise in employment and social security law.

It can be useful for initial market entry, for hiring a limited number of employees or for testing business opportunities in Bulgaria.

Legal considerations

Bulgarian law does not regulate EOR as a separate legal concept. It is therefore necessary to analyse the actual substance of the relationship between the provider, the foreign company and the employees.

Depending on how the work is organised, the model may have the features of temporary agency work within the meaning of Article 107p et seq. of the Labour Code. In that case the applicable statutory requirements must be met, including the registration requirements under the Employment Promotion Act.

Where the relationship is organised as an independent provision of services, the provider must genuinely organise the activity and be responsible for the agreed result. The name of the agreement alone is not sufficient to determine its legal classification.

2. Hiring through a temporary work agency

The temporary work agency is a model expressly regulated by Bulgarian employment law.

Under it, the agency concludes an employment contract with the employee and assigns him or her to a user undertaking to perform work under the terms and procedure laid down in the Labour Code.

This model may suit companies that need staff for a defined period, for a specific project or during a temporary increase in workload.

Advantages

Temporary agency work provides a statutory framework for the provision of workforce between separate undertakings.

It can be useful where a company has a specific, time-limited need for staff without building its own permanent structure for hiring and managing employees.

Legal considerations

The regime is set out in Article 107p et seq. of the Labour Code and is linked to the requirements of the Employment Promotion Act.

A temporary work agency must meet the statutory conditions for carrying out this activity, including registration with the Employment Agency.

The legislation lays down special rules on employment contracts, employees' rights, the relationship with the user undertaking and financial guarantees. There are also restrictions on the use of temporary agency work in certain cases and under certain conditions.

This model should therefore be planned in light of the specific staffing need and the applicable statutory limitations.

3. Setting up your own company in Bulgaria

A foreign company may set up a Bulgarian company that hires employees and carries on business in the country in its own right.

This is a more structured solution, which usually requires more initial organisation and ongoing administration but provides greater independence in managing the local activity.

Advantages

Having its own company allows the business to be the direct employer of the employees in Bulgaria and to manage the employment relationships through its own local structure.

It may be appropriate for a larger team, a long-term presence or plans for substantial operations in the country.

A local company can also make it easier to contract with Bulgarian clients and suppliers and to organise other business relationships locally.

Legal and administrative considerations

Setting up a company involves registration in the Commercial Register, arranging accounting services and complying with the applicable tax, social security and administrative obligations.

As an employer, the company must comply with the Labour Code, including the rules on concluding and terminating employment contracts, working time, leave, remuneration and health and safety at work.

The costs of maintaining the company and of its administrative and accounting services should also be budgeted for.

4. How to choose the right model

There is no universal solution that suits every foreign company. The choice depends on the specific business situation and on how the employees' work will be organised.

When the company wants to start with a limited number of employees

EOR can be a practical option if the aim is to start working in Bulgaria quickly without immediately setting up a local company. Before choosing it, however, it should be checked whether the specific model complies with the applicable regime for the provision of workforce or services.

When the need for staff is temporary

A temporary work agency may be a suitable solution for project-based work, a temporary increase in workload or other situations permitted under the Labour Code.

When a long-term presence is planned

Having its own company can give the business greater control and independence in managing employees and local operations. It is often an option worth considering for a larger team or a long-term business strategy.

When the activity has specific tax or regulatory features

Whichever model is chosen, it is important to assess in advance the tax consequences, the applicable employment law requirements and any regulatory obligations.

Particular attention should be paid to the foreign company's actual role in managing the employees and to whether its activity in Bulgaria may give rise to additional tax consequences, including permanent establishment issues.

5. What should be checked before starting?

Before choosing a specific model, a foreign company should clarify several key questions:

  • How many employees does it plan to hire, and for how long?
  • What will be the nature of their work?
  • Who will organise their day-to-day activity and set their tasks?
  • Do the employees need to work under the direct supervision of the foreign company?
  • Will they create intellectual property, and how will the rights in it be settled?
  • What are the tax and social security consequences of the chosen model?
  • What are the overall costs, including administrative fees and the costs of ending the arrangement?

The answers to these questions can help in choosing a structure that is both practical for the business and compliant with Bulgarian law.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company hire employees in Bulgaria without having a local company?

Yes. Depending on the specific situation, this can be done through an EOR provider or through a temporary work agency. The chosen model must comply with the applicable statutory requirements.

Are EOR and temporary agency work the same thing?

Not necessarily. EOR is a contractual and business model that is not regulated as a separate legal concept in Bulgaria. Temporary agency work is a statutory regime. Depending on how the relationship is actually organised, a particular EOR model may fall within the temporary agency work regime.

Can the foreign company itself manage the day-to-day work of employees hired through an EOR?

That depends on the specific model and the applicable legal regime. The allocation of functions and responsibilities must be carefully agreed, taking into account how the work is actually carried out.

When is it necessary to set up a Bulgarian company?

There is no universal rule requiring every foreign company that hires employees in Bulgaria to set up a local company. Whether it is necessary or advisable depends on the nature and scale of the activity and on the applicable tax and regulatory rules.

Which option is more cost-effective?

That depends on the number of employees, the period of employment, the provider's fees and the costs of maintaining your own company. The comparison should cover not only the initial costs but also the long-term administrative and operating costs.

Can the company later move from EOR to its own company?

Yes. A company can change the chosen model as its business in Bulgaria grows or as its local presence becomes longer-term. The transition should be planned so that the employment relationships, contracts and other applicable obligations are properly dealt with.
