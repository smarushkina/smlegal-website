Convertible Loan or Equity: Structuring a Start-up's First Funding Round

A comparison of the main early-stage funding instruments and what founders and investors in Bulgaria should look out for.

The first investment round often determines the company's future structure. The choice of instrument affects control, founder dilution and the terms of subsequent rounds. Here is a brief overview of the options most commonly used in Bulgarian practice.

Direct equity investment

The investor acquires shares in exchange for a cash contribution and the company's capital is increased. The advantages are clarity and the immediate acquisition of shareholder rights. The drawbacks are the need to value the company at an early stage and a heavier process — a shareholders' resolution, amendment of the articles and registration with the Commercial Register.

Convertible loan

The investor provides a loan which, upon a defined event — usually the next funding round — converts into shares at a discount or subject to a valuation cap. The instrument defers the valuation question and speeds up the deal. When structuring it, it is important to address the conversion mechanism, shareholder consent to the future capital increase and the consequences if the round never happens.

SAFE-type agreements

The SAFE model, popular in the US, is not a loan and carries no interest or maturity. Its economic logic can be achieved in Bulgaria, but the agreement must be adapted to national rules on capital increases. Mechanically applying foreign templates risks unenforceable provisions.

Variable capital company

The variable capital company introduced into the Bulgarian Commerce Act offers greater flexibility in admitting new shareholders and issuing different classes of shares. For certain start-ups it can be a good alternative to the classic limited liability company, especially where an employee option plan is planned.

What founders should watch

  • Liquidation preference and its type — participating or non-participating;
  • Veto rights and the composition of management bodies;
  • Anti-dilution protection and its mechanism;
  • Founder vesting and leaver provisions (good/bad leaver);
  • Tag-along and drag-along rights.
