Work Permits in Bulgaria: Single Permit or EU Blue Card?

Which route to choose when hiring a non-EU national in Bulgaria, what the conditions and quotas are, how the procedure works and which mistakes to avoid.

Labour shortages are leading more and more Bulgarian employers to hire people from outside the European Union. Non-EU nationals may, however, work in Bulgaria only on the basis of a permit issued under the Labour Migration and Labour Mobility Act and the Foreigners in the Republic of Bulgaria Act. Choosing the right route determines the timeline, the documents and the chances of success.

Citizens of the EU, the European Economic Area and Switzerland do not need a work permit. This article concerns everyone else.

1. The main routes at a glance

  • Single permit for residence and work — the general route for employing non-EU nationals;
  • EU Blue Card — for highly qualified professionals with a degree or relevant professional experience and a salary above the statutory threshold;
  • Intra-corporate transfer — for managers, specialists and trainees moved from a group company outside the EU;
  • Seasonal work — for limited periods in seasonal activities;
  • Posting — when a foreign employer sends its own employee to Bulgaria.

2. The single permit

The single permit is the most widely used route. It combines the right to reside and the right to work for a specific employer in one document.

Key conditions

  • Quota — the total number of non-EU nationals employed by the employer over the previous 12 months may not exceed 20% of its average headcount, or 35% for small and medium-sized enterprises (Article 7(1)(1) of the Labour Migration and Labour Mobility Act);
  • Equal treatment — working conditions and pay may not be less favourable than for Bulgarian employees in a comparable position;
  • Qualification — the employee must have the education and experience the position requires.

3. The EU Blue Card

The Blue Card is designed for highly qualified employment. It requires a higher education degree or, for certain IT occupations, proven professional experience, and a gross salary at least equal to the statutory threshold linked to the national average salary.

Holders enjoy more favourable conditions for changing employer, family reunification and mobility within the EU. See our detailed “EU Blue Card in Bulgaria: A Guide for Employers”.

4. Which route to choose

For highly qualified positions

If the candidate has a degree and the salary meets the threshold, the Blue Card is usually the better route, given its more favourable terms for the employee and their family.

For skilled workers without a degree

For skilled workers, technicians and other specialists without higher education, the single permit is usually the right route. The employer needs to keep track of the quota for non-EU nationals.

For employees already in the same group

Where the employee moves from a group company outside the EU, an intra-corporate transfer may be the best fit.

5. How the procedure works

  1. Assessment — choosing the route, checking the quota and the candidate's qualifications;
  2. Preparation — employment contract, job description, employer and employee documents, legalisation and translation;
  3. Filing — application to the Migration Directorate of the Ministry of Interior, with an opinion from the Employment Agency;
  4. Type D visa — after a positive decision, the employee obtains a long-stay visa from the Bulgarian embassy or consulate;
  5. Residence document — after arrival the employee receives the permit, and only then may start work.

Realistically, the whole procedure takes several months, so planning should start as soon as the candidate is selected.

6. Common mistakes

  • Starting work before the permit is issued — leading to penalties for both employer and employee;
  • Miscalculating the quota of non-EU nationals;
  • A salary below the Blue Card threshold, or below the level for comparable positions under the single permit;
  • Incomplete, untranslated or non-legalised documents from the country of origin;
  • Missing the renewal deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Who issues work permits for foreign nationals in Bulgaria?

Applications for a single permit or an EU Blue Card are filed with the Migration Directorate of the Ministry of Interior, and the Employment Agency gives an opinion on the employer and the terms of employment.

How many non-EU employees can an employer hire?

Non-EU nationals employed over the previous 12 months may not exceed 20% of the employer’s average headcount, or 35% for small and medium-sized enterprises.

What is the difference between the single permit and the Blue Card?

The Blue Card is for highly qualified professionals with a degree or relevant experience paid above the statutory threshold, and offers more favourable terms for changing employer and family reunification. The single permit is the general route for employing non-EU nationals.

How long does it take to get a work permit in Bulgaria?

Realistically, several months for the whole procedure, depending on document preparation, the authorities’ workload and the issuing of a type D visa.

Do EU citizens need a work permit in Bulgaria?

No. Citizens of the EU, the EEA and Switzerland have free access to the labour market, but must obtain a long-term residence certificate if they stay longer than three months.
