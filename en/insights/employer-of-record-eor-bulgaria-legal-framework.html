Employer of Record (EOR) in Bulgaria: Legal Framework, Advantages and Key Considerations

How foreign companies can hire employees in Bulgaria without setting up their own company — the legal framework, advantages and key considerations of the Employer of Record (EOR) model.

Employer of Record (EOR) allows foreign companies to hire employees in Bulgaria without setting up their own company or branch. Although Bulgarian law does not regulate EOR as a separate legal concept, there are legal mechanisms through which such a model can be organised. The choice of structure depends mainly on how functions are divided between the service provider and the client company, and on how the work is actually performed.

This article looks at the applicable legal framework, the main advantages and features of the EOR model, and the questions foreign companies should consider when hiring staff in Bulgaria.

What is an Employer of Record (EOR)?

Employer of Record is a hiring model in which a local company acts as the employer under the employment relationship, while the employee performs work for the benefit of another company — usually a foreign one.

The EOR provider concludes the employment contract with the employee and takes on the related administrative and employer obligations. These include paying the salary, calculating and remitting taxes and social security contributions, administering leave and complying with the applicable requirements of employment law.

The model is of particular interest to international companies that wish to hire specialists in Bulgaria, build a remote team or explore the local market before deciding whether to establish their own presence in the country.

In this way the company can rely on local employer and administrative infrastructure without immediately taking on all the organisational commitments involved in setting up and managing a Bulgarian company.

Legal framework for EOR in Bulgaria

The Bulgarian Labour Code and the Employment Promotion Act do not contain specific rules on Employer of Record. This does not, however, rule out the provision of such services. The legal structure must comply with the existing provisions governing employment relationships and the provision of workforce.

In practice, what matters is how functions are divided between the provider and the client and, in particular, who organises, directs and supervises the employee's work.

1. Temporary agency work

Where the foreign company (the client) in fact directs and supervises the employee's work, the arrangement may fall within the temporary agency work regime under Article 107p et seq. of the Labour Code.

In that case the provider must be registered with the Employment Agency as a temporary work agency. The law also requires the provider to maintain security in the form of insurance or a bank guarantee of approximately EUR 102,258.

The regime also sets certain requirements for the use of assigned staff:

  • The employment contract is concluded for a specific task or to replace an absent employee.
  • The number of employees assigned as temporary agency workers may not exceed 30% of the total headcount of the user undertaking.
  • The temporary work agency and the user undertaking bear the joint and several liability provided for by law for certain obligations towards the employee.

This regime provides a statutory framework for the provision of staff, while imposing specific conditions on the provider and the user undertaking. When planning long-term employment or a larger team, it is important to assess in advance whether these limitations are compatible with the company's needs.

2. Provision of services

In certain cases the relationship between the provider and the foreign client can be organised as a services agreement.

This approach requires the provider to retain independence in organising and managing the work of the persons it employs, to be responsible for performing the agreed tasks and to deliver a defined result to the client.

Under a genuine services agreement that does not amount to the provision of temporary work, the provider is not required to register with the Employment Agency on that ground. The employment contracts with the employees may also be for an indefinite term.

It is important, however, that the contractual structure reflects how the work is actually organised. Simply labelling the relationship as a services agreement is not sufficient if in practice the client exercises direct control and employer functions over the employees. In such cases it should be analysed whether the temporary agency work regime applies.

The choice between the two approaches should therefore be based on the specific business model, not on the contractual form alone.

Advantages of the EOR model for foreign companies

Employer of Record offers a number of practical advantages for companies planning to hire staff in Bulgaria.

Faster entry into the Bulgarian market

One of the main advantages is the ability to hire local employees without first setting up a Bulgarian company or branch. This allows the company to start working with a local team with fewer initial organisational steps.

Access to local specialists

The EOR model allows international companies to hire employees in Bulgaria and benefit from the qualified local workforce without initially building their own administrative structure.

Administrative convenience

The provider takes on the administration of the employment relationships, payroll processing and the related tax and social security obligations. This reduces the need for the foreign company to organise these processes itself.

Flexibility when expanding

The model may be suitable for building small teams, hiring individual specialists or gradually expanding a presence in Bulgaria. It allows the company to assess the potential of the local market before investing in its own corporate structure.

Lower initial set-up costs

Using an EOR can save part of the costs of incorporating, administering and maintaining a local company. The provider's fees and the overall cost of employment should, however, be taken into account.

Key considerations

Like any cross-border hiring model, EOR requires a prior assessment of several legal and practical questions. They are not necessarily an obstacle to using it, but they matter for the choice of structure and for the long-term stability of the arrangement.

Choosing the right legal structure

The main question is whether the model should be organised as temporary agency work or as the provision of services.

The two approaches have different requirements and consequences. It is therefore important to clarify at the planning stage who will direct the employees, how responsibilities will be allocated and how day-to-day work will be organised.

Intellectual property

Where employees create software, developments, documentation or other intellectual property, the rights in the results of their work need to be settled in advance.

Since the employer under the employment contract is the local provider and not the foreign client, the agreements should contain appropriate clauses on the use, licensing and transfer of the relevant rights, to the extent permitted by the applicable law.

Tax aspects and permanent establishment

Hiring employees in Bulgaria through an EOR does not automatically mean that the foreign company will have a permanent establishment in the country. The question should, however, be examined in light of the employees' specific functions and authority, the nature of the activity and the applicable double taxation treaty.

Particular care is needed where the employees take part in managing the business or in negotiating or concluding contracts on behalf of the foreign company.

EOR or your own company in Bulgaria?

The choice between EOR and setting up your own company depends on the company's plans, the number of employees, the duration of the activity and the degree of control it wishes to exercise over the local team.

EOR may be a suitable solution for companies that:

  • plan to hire one or a few employees in Bulgaria;
  • wish to build a remote team;
  • are exploring the opportunities of the Bulgarian market;
  • wish to start operating without immediately setting up a local company.

Your own company may be more appropriate for a long-term presence, a growing team, a need for direct employer control or plans for independent business activity in Bulgaria.

The two approaches should not be seen as mutually exclusive. In some situations a company may start with an EOR and later, as its activity expands, move to its own company.

The most suitable option depends on the specific business goals and on a legal and tax assessment of the planned model.

Frequently asked questions about Employer of Record in Bulgaria

Is Employer of Record legal in Bulgaria?

Yes. Bulgarian law allows contractual and organisational models through which a local company employs staff for the needs of a foreign client. The specific structure must comply with the applicable rules on temporary agency work or on the provision of services.

Is registration with the Employment Agency required?

Registration is required where the activity amounts to temporary agency work within the meaning of the Labour Code and the Employment Promotion Act. Under a genuine services agreement that falls outside that regime, no such registration is required on that ground.

Can a foreign company set up its own EOR company in Bulgaria?

Yes. Foreign individuals and legal entities may hold shares in Bulgarian companies. If the company will carry out temporary agency work, it must meet the statutory requirements for registration and security.

Can employees be hired on indefinite-term contracts through an EOR?

That depends on the legal structure. The temporary agency work regime provides specific grounds for concluding the employment contract. Under a genuine provision-of-services model, employment contracts may be for an indefinite term, subject to the general requirements of employment law.

Does a foreign company need to register a company in Bulgaria if it uses an EOR?

Not necessarily. One of the main practical purposes of EOR is to allow a company to hire local staff through a Bulgarian provider without first setting up its own company. It should be assessed separately, however, whether the specific activity and the presence of the employees give rise to other registration or tax obligations.
